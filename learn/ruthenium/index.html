Ruthenium: element 44

By QuantaPhi · AI-assisted draft by Codex · Published October 4, 2026

What is ruthenium? Ruthenium is a chemical element with symbol Ru and atomic number 44. It is a transition metal in group 8, period 5, and is solid at room temperature. [1]

Read the numbers correctly

Every ruthenium nucleus has 44 protons. A neutral ruthenium atom also has 44 electrons. Isotopes share the proton count but differ in neutron count. [2] [3]

Three quantities that are easy to confuse
QuantityRuthenium exampleMeaning
Atomic number44Protons in the nucleus
Mass number102 in ruthenium-102Protons plus neutrons in one isotope
Relative atomic mass101.07Average for the element; not a neutron count

A worked example

For ruthenium-102, subtract the proton count from the mass number: 102 − 44 = 58 neutrons. The 102 names this isotope. You cannot calculate an individual isotope’s neutron count by subtracting 44 from the decimal 101.07.

Where is ruthenium useful?

Ruthenium is used in some electrical contacts and chip resistors. It can harden platinum and palladium alloys, and ruthenium compounds can act as catalysts. Ruthenium oxide is used in electrochemical anodes for chlorine production. These uses depend on the material’s form and the particular application. [1]

How to investigate a claim

“Ruthenium is used in electronics” is a starting point. A fuller search asks which component, whether the material is metallic ruthenium or a compound, and what source supports that specific use. A statement about one form should not silently become a statement about every ruthenium material.

Check your understanding: does a charged ruthenium atom become a new element?

No. Changing the electron count changes charge. Changing the proton count changes the element. Changing only the neutron count changes the isotope.
