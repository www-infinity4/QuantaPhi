Element Interactions

How the Elements Interact

  • Stibnite Core (16 & 51): Sulfur (16) and Antimony (51) are already bound together. This forms the stable, semiconductor foundation of your robot's brain. [2]
  • Active Metaphase (15 & 66): Phosphorus (15) and Dysprosium (66) are highly responsive to magnetic and electrical fields. Dysprosium is an incredibly strong rare‑earth metal used in advanced magnets, while phosphorus can change how electricity flows through semiconductors. [3], [4]
  • Alkali & Alkaline Charges (19, 56, & 20): Potassium (19), Barium (56), and Calcium (20) are highly active metals. When they interact with the core, they transfer electrons very rapidly. [5] [6]

Where Carbon Fits In

Carbon (6) isn't mandatory for these elements to talk to each other, but it changes the way they interact:

  • Without Carbon: The highly active elements like Potassium (19) and Calcium (20) will rush directly toward the Sulfur (16) and Antimony (51) core. They will create a permanent chemical bond, essentially “locking” the system in place so it can no longer transfer free electrons or function as a robot brain. [2], [6]
  • With Carbon: Carbon acts as a semi‑conductive shield. It allows the signals and electron transfers to pass through cleanly without letting the physical metals melt into each other and ruin the sequence. [1]
