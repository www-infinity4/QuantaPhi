ESP32 · GRB research protocol

Working hypothesis notebook. “GRB” is used here for the proposed research project; the astronomical term gamma-ray burst has a distinct established definition.

Detector reality check: An ESP32 does not detect gamma rays on its own. Its GPIO or PCNT hardware counts electrical edges, not photon energies. Gamma measurements require an external radiation detector with a compatible conditioned output, appropriate shielding/calibration and safe power electronics. Do not connect a high-voltage detector directly to ESP32 pins. Espressif pulse counter ↗

What exactly is being measured?

Electrical pulses: signal transitions at a microcontroller input. Ionizing gamma photons: high-energy electromagnetic radiation detected through interaction with a suitable instrument. Cosmic gamma-ray bursts: brief astrophysical outbursts monitored by observatories such as NASA Fermi. Neither molecular motion nor everyday digital pulses establish a gamma-ray burst. NASA gamma rays ↗

Project proposal: test whether changes in a properly instrumented count rate differ from background and independently recorded events. “A burst living in each molecule” remains a hypothesis with no verified detector result or established scientific definition.

Controlled measurement sequence

  1. Specify the scientific question, expected observation, alternative explanation and measurement units before collecting data.
  2. Use a commercially documented, appropriate and calibrated gamma-sensitive detector or a certified educational simulator. Obtain professional review for any high-voltage or radiation apparatus.
  3. Connect only a low-voltage, isolated, conditioned detector pulse output to the ESP32 according to the instrument's specifications. The ESP32 records pulses; it does not classify gamma radiation by itself.
  4. Log UTC timestamp, device model, detector type, pulse count, time window, location category (not private address), calibration reference and source provenance. Preserve raw counts and firmware version.
  5. Record background and control runs, repeat observations, compare statistical variation and note uncertainty. A spike alone does not establish a cosmic GRB.
  6. Cross-check proposed astronomical events against independent NASA GCN notices ↗ and Fermi records. Preserve counterexamples and failed runs.
  7. Publish only aggregated, consented measurements with uncertainty and limitations. Never turn private images, personal beliefs or someone’s behavior into claimed sensor output.

Human and spiritual limits

Even if a detector records pulses, that cannot establish another person's thoughts, identity, or intentions. No reading proves or disproves a spiritual experience. Bubbles should respond to personal concerns without inventing conclusions about individuals.

Verification gates for Bubbles

Source checked → firmware test → calibrated instrument check → independent comparison → public readback. Each stage requires its own evidence. The current Quick Card is research text, not a connected detector, automated head counter, or verified live feed.
